# Revised DMP method

## Classical discrete DMP

The planar reference path is generated by the stable transformation system

\[
\dot{v}=\alpha_z\left[\beta_z(g-y)-v\right]+A f(s)+C(y,v),
\qquad \dot{y}=v,
\]

with canonical phase

\[
\dot{s}=-\alpha_s s.
\]

The nonlinear forcing term is a normalized radial-basis-function expansion:

\[
f(s)=s\frac{\sum_i w_i\psi_i(s)}{\sum_i\psi_i(s)},
\qquad
\psi_i(s)=\exp[-h_i(s-c_i)^2].
\]

Weights are obtained by ridge regression against the forcing required to
reproduce the demonstrated trajectory. The demonstration is parameterized with
a minimum-jerk timing law so its endpoint velocity agrees with the point-goal
attractor.

## Spatial generalization

The forcing term is learned in a local frame whose first axis points from the
demonstrated start to its goal. For a new start and goal, matrix `A` rotates the
local forcing vector into the new start-goal direction and scales it by the
ratio of the new and demonstrated start-goal distances. This supports
translation, rotation, and uniform spatial scaling without dividing the x and
y channels by possibly zero coordinate displacements.

## Obstacle coupling

The obstacle-aware rollout uses the Hoffmann et al. velocity-dependent steering
term. Nearby obstacles in front of the rover generate an acceleration
perpendicular to velocity:

\[
C=\gamma Rv\,\theta\exp(-\beta\theta),
\]

where `R` turns velocity away from the obstacle and `theta` is the angle between
the velocity and obstacle direction. A finite influence distance and
acceleration limit are engineering safeguards. The common path-clearance screen
and Bullet contact detection remain independent of this coupling term.

## Interpretation

The DMP does not identify why the demonstrator curved. It reproduces the
demonstrated geometric pattern through its learned forcing term. Obstacle
avoidance comes from the separate state-dependent coupling term. Therefore the
project compares both the unmodified spatially scaled DMP and the same DMP with
obstacle feedback.

## Evaluation policy

All planners receive matched starts, maps, controllers, rover geometry, and
completion rules. Results report individual measurements instead of a tuned
aggregate score:

- safe completion, rejection, safety-stop, and contact rates;
- minimum Bullet and grid clearance;
- planning time and controller execution time;
- drive duration and distance;
- cross-track RMSE and 95th-percentile error;
- maximum curvature, bending energy, and integrated turn demand.
