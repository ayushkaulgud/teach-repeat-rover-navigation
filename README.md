## November 4 stress update

The baseline already exceeded the original 60-attempt target: 180 planning attempts on three recorded route segments. The new stress suite adds **408 scenario attempts and 333 simulated drives**. Some starts are repeated under different safety, controller, friction, and rover settings, so these are paired scenario tests, not 408 independent outdoor observations. All route poses and obstacle returns still come from the [Freiburg Campus recording](data/freiburg_campus/README.md); displaced starts and repeat drives are simulated.

We widened starts to **-2.4, 0, and +2.4 m** and headings to **-60, 0, and +60 degrees**. Each of the four planners was attempted at all nine starts on each of three routes, for 108 attempts. The original 0.15 m grid path screen produced **93 completions, 9 pre-drive rejections, and 6 Bullet contacts**. All contacts were on the first route at -2.4 m with -60 or 0 degree heading. This exposes a mismatch between the map clearance proxy and the driven Husky footprint or tracked trajectory.

| Safety setting on the same 108 attempts | Completed | Rejected before drive | Safety stops | Bullet contacts |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Original 0.15 m path screen | 93 | 9 | 0 | 6 |
| Larger 0.38 m path screen | 72 | 36 | 0 | 0 |
| Original screen plus 0.15 m Bullet proximity stop | 93 | 9 | 6 | 0 |

The larger screen removed contacts by rejecting all first-route attempts, including drives that the original setting completed. The proximity check converted the six contacts into safety stops without changing the 93 completions. The check stops the simulated rover immediately, without a braking-distance model. This is a simulator safety result, not a validated physical stopping distance.

On the first route, an adaptive-lookahead controller was tested on the nine wide starts. It still had **21 completions, 9 rejections, and 6 contacts** across 36 attempts; it did not fix the unsafe starts. A separate matched check used three standard starts per method. The original Husky, adaptive controller, low-friction wheel setting, and class-built four-wheel URDF each had **9 completions and 3 classical-DMP rejections** over 12 attempts. Their median drive times among completions were **35.43, 35.00, 35.76, and 38.22 s**, respectively. This small matched check does not establish controller or hardware superiority. The class rover is an uncalibrated simulation model. PyBullet wheel friction of 0.2 is a low-grip proxy, not a measurement of outdoor wheel slip.

## November 4 results

The benchmark now covers Freiburg scan windows 300–549, 1050–1299, and 1800–2049. The later two taught routes are 30.60 m and 20.92 m. Each route uses the same four methods and 15 offset starts. The 550–799 and 800–1049 windows failed the 0.15 m route-clearance screen and were excluded from drive trials.

| Method | Completed | Rejected before drive | Bullet contacts |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Direct waypoints | 45/45 | 0 | 0 |
| Local A* | 45/45 | 0 | 0 |
| Classical DMP | 30/45 | 15 | 0 |
| Obstacle-aware DMP | 45/45 | 0 | 0 |

There were **180 planning attempts, 165 simulated drives, and 165 completed drives** across these selected recorded segments. No driven trial registered an obstacle contact. The classical DMP's 15 rejected paths all occurred on the first segment. These are outcomes under a static map, common safety screen, and exact simulated pose; they do not establish physical rover performance. [Combined summary](results/nov4/summary.json), [outcomes figure](results/nov4/outcomes.png), [second-route trials](results/second_route/trials.csv), and [third-route trials](results/third_route/trials.csv) preserve the measurements.

ICP was also evaluated on **34 recorded laser scans held out from the map**, with two injected pose offsets per scan. Across 68 registrations, the median position difference from logged odometry fell from 0.640 m to 0.149 m; the 90th-percentile final difference was 0.295 m. Logged odometry is not surveyed ground truth. ICP now selects its map neighborhood from the initial pose estimate rather than the logged reference pose. [ICP summary](results/icp_validation/summary.json), [trial data](results/icp_validation/trials.csv), and [figure](results/icp_validation/errors.png) show the full results.

An offline replay feeds 68 ICP pose estimates from these real scans to the same path follower used in Bullet. Direct waypoints, local A*, and obstacle-aware DMP passed the clearance screen, giving 204 controller-command updates. Classical DMP was screened out before replay. The median position difference from logged odometry was 0.025 m both before and after ICP once recorded odometry increments propagated the pose; this segment does not show an overall accuracy gain from ICP in sequential replay. Commands were computed but not applied to the robot or a Bullet drive. [Replay summary](results/icp_controller_replay/summary.json), [trials](results/icp_controller_replay/trials.csv), and [figure](results/icp_controller_replay/controller_replay.png) preserve the result.

## October baseline setup

The [Freiburg Campus log](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/de754aaf-ddfc-479c-a193-dc77c7005816) was recorded outdoors by Cyrill Stachniss and Giorgio Grisetti on July 14, 2004 with a Pioneer 2AT and SICK LMS291 laser. MIT lists it as CC0. The raw CARMEN log in [`data/freiburg_campus/raw.log.gz`](data/freiburg_campus/raw.log.gz) contains 15,683 front-laser scans and recorded poses. [Dataset details](data/freiburg_campus/README.md) include its source and checksum.

[`src/recorded_demo.py`](src/recorded_demo.py) uses scans 300–549 as a 29.59 m taught route, and nearby laser returns to make a 0.25 m obstacle grid. Four planners start from the same 15 offset poses. [PyBullet](https://github.com/bulletphysics/bullet3) drives a Husky model at 120 Hz through static geometry reconstructed from those returns. This is a surrogate vehicle: the recording robot was a Pioneer 2AT. Navigation uses exact simulated pose; the ICP check is separate.

| Method | Completed | Rejected | Collided | Median plan time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Direct waypoints | 15/15 | 0/15 | 0/15 | 0.12 ms |
| Local A* | 15/15 | 0/15 | 0/15 | 1.59 ms |
| Classical DMP | 0/15 | 15/15 | 0/15 | 7.73 ms |
| Obstacle-aware DMP | 15/15 | 0/15 | 0/15 | 10.15 ms |

The classical DMP's candidate paths failed the 0.15 m map-clearance rule and were rejected before execution. The other planners completed all starts in this **one** route segment. There were no Bullet obstacle contacts. Completion means ending within 0.65 m of the logged goal; it does not imply centimeter-scale accuracy. Full measurements are in [`metrics.json`](results/recorded/metrics.json) and each trial is in [`trials.csv`](results/recorded/trials.csv).

A held-out recorded scan was aligned to nearby returns with ICP after an injected 0.64 m pose error. Its final displacement from **logged odometry** was 0.044 m, with 0.047 m map residual. Logged odometry is not independent surveyed ground truth.

## Limits

Laser returns from the recorded segments define static maps. Moving people and cars in the original scene are frozen into the grid. The tests vary starting pose, route segment, controller, rover model, and a simulated wheel-friction setting, not measured terrain or weather. The 0.42 m footprint radius is a path-screening proxy, and grid clearance has 0.25 m resolution. Results should not be presented as physical rover performance. Next: apply ICP poses in a closed-loop drive, assess excluded route windows, and calibrate the rover model to intended hardware.
